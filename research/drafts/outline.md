# Section-level argument outline

Status: source-bound outline completed before prose  
Date: 2026-09-01

## Abstract

Problem: one recognizable object enters four incompatible social classifications. Method: material-culture/object-biography study, visual analysis limited to public appearance, institutional discourse analysis, media theory, and legal-text comparison. Thesis revised from “movement causes meanings” to “visible transformation is an enabling affordance whose meanings are socially produced.” Limitation: sparse local ethnography and uneven archives.

## 1. Introduction: One Object, Four Social Lives

- Open with conflicting documentary situations rather than danger or spectacle: 1951 economic report, 1994 cultural encyclopedia, interaction aesthetics, 1971 legal notice.
- Question: how does visible transformation become craft knowledge, aesthetic form, public performance, and legal evidence?
- Gap: origin narratives collapse social history into a single birthplace claim; design talk isolates form; legal summaries flatten statutes into advice.
- Claim C01/C02. Sources: `lardizabal1951`, `ccp1994`, `rosswensveen2010`, `customs1971`, `riddall1991`.

## 2. Method: Following the Object Across Social Worlds

- Object biography follows reclassification rather than assuming essence. Claim C03.
- Boundary object is qualified: recognizable across worlds, but the worlds need not cooperate. Claim C04.
- Five evidence states: directly documented, visually observed, institutionally framed, historically contested, authorial interpretation. Claim C05.
- Search/rights/AI limits; abstract-only not full text; no technical extraction.

- Lasanas only documents Philippine legal vocabulary, not origin. Claim C24.

## 8. Counterarguments and Limits

1. Design determinism: reject sufficient cause; retain enabling affordance (`vandijkrietveld2017`, `ramstead2016`).
2. Internet novelty: continuity is insufficient; rupture/reinvention open (`lardizabal1951`, `jenkins2006`).
3. Legal controversy as “weapon only”: statutes do use movement language, but aesthetics does not excuse or explain away regulation (`usc1241`, `riddall1991`).
4. Heritage as later branding: institutional/tourism discourse can constitute the heritage it names (`smith2012`, `gonzalez2017`, `taalelpasubat2013`, `yasa2024`).
5. Evidence bias: English and online materials overrepresented; maker interviews, Filipino-language archives, museum files, and original 1969 brochure remain gaps. Claim C28.

## 9. Conclusion: What Moves When the Object Moves?

- Answer at category level: region to nation/global image; function to aesthetic event; practice to public performance; design to legal evidence.
- The object exposes instability of imposed categories, not a final truth. Claim C29.
- Transferable implication: dynamic objects should be studied through movement, institutions, and social itineraries together.
